# TNOA-to-ecology: wind, pollinator sorting, and interaction reallocation

**Date:** 2026-10-19. **Status:** exploratory, source-first, outcome comparison not opened under this protocol. **Owner:** TNOA ecological extension sidecar, NOT TNOA Paper 1, C2, REC, PolliPi or InsePi V13.

## Biological question (not an observer-accuracy endpoint)

**Does wind uniformly reduce pollination activity, or redistribute which taxa interact, and how they move among flowers, with context-dependent consequences?**

Keep three distinct *ecological* outcomes (do not combine them into a composite):
1. **Interaction quantity**: number of insects/events per comparable, independently defined sampling effort (only where true zero-effort windows are present).
2. **Interaction identity**: proportional Diptera vs Hymenoptera among *recorded* visitation/interaction events, conditional on a standardized sampling unit (composition only; cannot infer total abundance without sampled zero windows).
3. **Behavioral allocation**: visits per fixed observation period and between-inflorescence / between-plant movement by identified visitors (not equivalent to effective pollen transfer).

A *positive* physical wind process is not automatically TNOA's N (nuisance). Wind may alter real insect behavior AND observation detection. Keep biological state and observation-process pathways distinct. This study investigates the former; no causal claim about detection without a reference observation system.

- Dryad https://doi.org/10.5061/dryad.4qrfj6qpf (page published 2026-05-15; original observations 2023–24).

## Discovery vs test and comparisons

These published sources already report wind associations; this is an **exploratory secondary synthesis**, not a new preregistered confirmation. Freeze the following *new comparative questions* before reading source rows:

**Q1: Ecological quantity vs composition.** Do wind-associated changes in absolute counts and observed taxonomic composition have the same direction or different directions within the study's site/date/plant coverage? Compare within-source estimates, not claims about one global ecosystem rate.

**Q2: Between-system sign portability.** For a common, meaning-aligned order contrast, does a wind-conditioned Diptera-vs-Hymenoptera ratio show consistent direction after site, flowering plant and temperature controls? Treat source as the independent replication unit, not taxa, sites or frames. A failed sign transport is biologically informative but no causal habitat mediator is identified by two studies.

**Q3: Ecological behavior trade-off.** Within the Balfour bee data, does higher wind covary with lower per-bee flower-visitation speed and greater between-inflorescence movement? Do NOT call inflorescence switching a confirmed interplant pollen-transfer event; discuss possible consequences only.

**Q4: Environment × resource context.** Test wind interaction with plant family or elevation only if within-context wind range and independent survey support are adequate (>=3 survey dates and >=2 wind bins per context). Otherwise HOLD rather than automatically pooling incompatible contexts.

## Primary inference design

- Unit of ecological sampling: independently timed transect/census, not individual insect visit rows; individual bee sequences only support conditional behavioral outcomes.
- Balfour: preserve zero-record transects and effort; temperature, sun, date, location and focal plant are nuisance/confounding covariates (in modeling terminology), not TNOA N process labels.
- Coates: sample ID × date/elevation/site is the unit for count composition; avoid treating 5,133 interaction rows as independent surveys. Group phylogenetically coarse orders to Diptera/Hymenoptera/Coleoptera where available, and exclude unknown orders with explicit denominator.
- Within each source: report raw proportions, wind support, standard errors/intervals with source-appropriate block/day/plant clustering if identifiable, and leave-one-site/date robustness. Study sample size is the number of independent survey periods, never the number of raw events.
- Negative controls: plant/bee/source-group exclusion; compare within-date/site wind support, excluding groups with no variation rather than inferring a null effect.
- **Cross-study**: report source-specific standardized signed contrasts and their support, with no random-effects meta-analysis on fewer than 3 independent sources; do not infer global consistency from two studies.
- No causal wind effect, universal pollinator mechanism, flower fitness, crop yield, or climate-change extrapolation without appropriate independent evidence.
