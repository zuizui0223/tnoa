Dear Editors of *Methods in Ecology and Evolution*,

Please consider our Research Article, **“When should an ecological sensor refuse to decide? Target–nuisance–observability–abstention for process-preserving ecological sensing.”**

The manuscript presents TNOA, a process-preserving sensor-decision architecture for ecological observation. The method addresses a recurring problem in automated ecological sensing: low target evidence can reflect biological absence, nuisance processes, inadequate measurement support, missing attribution, or observer limitations, while target and nuisance processes may genuinely co-occur. TNOA therefore keeps positive target, target-coupled, nuisance and observability evidence distinct; permits target+nuisance coexistence; requires independent evidence if absence is to be certified; and retains abstention when the available channels do not justify a unique biological statement.

We believe the manuscript fits *Methods in Ecology and Evolution* as a Research Article because the primary contribution is methodological rather than an application result. The architecture is analysed in a controlled closed-world experiment over six dimensionless process/observation coordinates. Observer rules and decision contracts were frozen before final measurement, negative generations were retained, and the final phase-surface analysis comprised 30,625 registered coordinates and 5,880,000 synthetic worlds. The paper explicitly does not claim field detection accuracy, calibrated field absence, or universal quantitative thresholds.

The manuscript also distinguishes its contribution from neighbouring methods. It does not claim to invent abstention, imperfect-detection correction, set-valued prediction, open-set recognition, evidence conflict representation, process/observation separation, sensor fusion or adaptive sampling. The contribution is the integrated ecological sensor-decision contract and its frozen dimensionless decision geometry. A transferability analysis maps the same process/evidence roles to wildlife camera traps, passive acoustics, nest/feeding monitoring and phenology cameras without claiming that fitted thresholds transfer unchanged.

Reproducibility and claim control are part of the method package. Locked results are tied to workflow runs, execution commits, artifact hashes and a paper manifest. Quantitative figures are generated only from pinned locked source artifacts. Code required for peer review will be provided through the journal's reviewer-facing mechanism or an anonymised/private repository, and the submitted code carries an open-source licence.
